__NEWSECTIONLINK__The [Technology Team](/tech-docs/techvolunteers) is working on a redesign of the RequestWiki interface to make the wiki request process clearer, easier to follow, and more user-friendly.

A demo of the proposed new RequestWiki wizard is available at:

[https://meta.mirabeta.org/wiki/Special:RequestWiki](https://meta.mirabeta.org/wiki/Special:RequestWiki)

We would like to hear feedback from the community on the proposed design. In particular, we would appreciate feedback on the usability, clarity, navigation, and overall experience of the new wizard.

Please note that submissions on Beta will not actually work. The page is provided solely as a demo of the new wizard design and is not connected to the production RequestWiki request process.

Please provide any feedback you have on the discussion. The Technology Team will review the feedback and use it to refine the redesign before it is deployed to production.

Thank you,

This started as a direct reply to Universal Omega ^ on a change and went to getting all my thoughts out in one go, bear with me.

I think high-fidelity options to figure out if something existing is available would be great, and ideally something easier than WikiDiscover, perhaps in the vein of a cache of names/databases (and managewiki descriptions?) to run a quick 'does this greatly overlap with something else' check. --- `{{ {{Collapse top|1=Lengthy broader tangent on the queue}} }}`
There are a few things here which are probably suited to split into different channels or discussions depending on interest.

From the community end I have been chewing on [User:Raidarr/Wiki request primer](https://meta.miraheze.org/wiki/User:Raidarr/Wiki_request_primer) because there is really no way to efficiently stick what people need to know into a small box, nor does linking all the policies bode well for giving people an abridged easy understanding of what they're getting into. This is something I'd actually like to frontload before the queue even, once the body matures. The lower section for example also contains information of niche importance but what would be better is if there was some way easier than a really long TOC for people to skim for common factors they should know a little more information about like a mini FAQ.

Some form of this would deliver necessary information and yet not oblige people to read a full half dozen policies in one sitting for which retention is very poor I am sure, things like Dormancy Policy come as a surprise and [wiki governance policy](https://meta.miraheze.org/wiki/wiki_governance_policy) remains an opaque thing to many and aren't even in the top 3 as far as platform crucial readings. I've come to prioritize working out this initial information delivery.

Probably the 'at least two sentences' will need a wording adjustment because its rather impractical for two sentences to hit 350 characters and is a point where we could abridge essentially the top 3 things. Different wiki creators phrase it differently but my top 3 wants are what is it, how will you cover it, and risk (what are they and how will you handle them). That's what I personally want, sometimes this is easier to convey than others and if I am reasonably confident on all 3 I am prone to say why not, which might be part of why some wiki creators have a reputation for strictness and I am considered generally quite lenient. It is tangents that draw out my stricter eye for which the primer stage I think could be helpful so people in good faith can minimize delays. If people skip that well-curated step that's on them. If they skip 'please read every global policy outright' plus our less prominent reference material we hope they might know and just don't come back after we didn't really sell sticking around, well to fix that you're asking for defiance of human nature and tbh we have done that for a while as a platform. "I have read and accept Content Policy, Dormancy Policy, Wiki Governance Policy and Global Conduct Policy, and by requesting a wiki, I agree to all our global policies." is a compromise of when the process became stricter and we started wanting that; wherever you put it, if its part of the oh btw read all of this approach, I think it just won't sink in the way we want.

"The purpose of this wiki is..." could probably resurrect the long bounced around tag cloud idea since it pretty much is just category with a mild twist. And/or something explicitly governance related since that is not always clear and I have long wanted this aspect to be more clear to users. If you're asking for something community scoped then I treat it as a community wiki and any designation like 'owner' is a vanity that does not override how we treat typical public community wikis, and I would put something otherwise under quite high scrutiny. But there is good cause for more restrictive management setups. Maybe this is a step for when we are more into preloaded templates, config etc. I remain an advocate of a broad-ranging community survey as our best precedented way to ask on these matters and reduce how many RfFs will be needed to get a vibe check on what to prioritize and where to take things. I would very much enjoy seeing it later this month or in next month and would be willing to offer an eye for setting it up.

I'm not certain how it currently works but as a final post-submission step perhaps people can be redirected to view their request, its status be prominent, and an echo notification made as a 'ding! here's the wiki' with a highly visible link both there and following approval. The link might even be part of 'this request has been approved:'. At the risk of an impossibly wishy detour I think this would over time mesh well with a rethink of the queue screen to obsolete the need of the third party wrangler for reviewers and to better support post-submission change like if the domain later adjusts over Phorge so the request can better serve as the canonical source of truth (not great for ancient wikis but might help the new generation). I've also tried intermittently to integrate scope changes into requests since there is no intuitive central process to know this and we're not going to search Phorge or all SR history unless we have extremely specific reason to.

Coming back around it may be interesting to have the form itself offer aspects of the primer as a more eloquent integrated reference, but this seems like a lot to do upfront and might be something we need to roadmap out rather than delay progress. But it would be good imo to separate the 'what are you doing' from the 'how will it be realized' elements so people have every opportunity to come across dealbreakers and ask questions before they worry about preloaded templates if that is not meant to be. At this stage being able to save progress and encourage consultation would be nice. I would very much like to support and encourage more 'intentional' requests. This may also address the intermittent occasion of 'how do I do this' or 'I would like to do...' sorts of questions that are easily lost and can be hard to follow up.

All of this came out in a stream of thought so let me make this a pitch to consider if we want to more broadly solicit input in where to go, perhaps put these thoughts on staff wiki, and work them into a roadmap or just abstract goals to work to which broader survey can help us refine. Some of this is just tweaks or separate discussion but I'm really just putting a whole approach upfront here. Apologies for train-of-thought delivery. `{{ {{Collapse bottom}} }}`

And now why not, where I am on the redesign itself.

I just like the incumbent approach of having one screen more as a matter of taste, I don't like breaking things into tiny click and move on steps. An overview at the end is useful for this approach but feels broadly less useful than the overall 'fill it then look it over where I can edit on the spot' approach. If nobody else cares about this then I guess I defer. The funny thing about the 'work on mobile' comment is this approach is pretty much optimal for mobile.

"Wiki options" and "Additional information" imo are both too limited to be single screens and would be more useful in a single one. Only if we better carve out wiki options with preloads and things of that nature would both steps be useful.

Perhaps it would be worth considering more flexible ability to bounce between screens in case you just want to pop back to step 1 real quick from step 4 or 5 without needing to be at the end (which is great in offering this with edit buttons; perhaps it might benefit from 'return to review' merging with this function).
